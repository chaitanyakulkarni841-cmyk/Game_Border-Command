# How Border Command evolved

[Portfolio index](../README.md)

These stages reconstruct the actual development sequence from the preceding work. They are **development milestones**, not published GitHub releases or independently measured usability improvements.

| Stage | Product focus | Change recorded | Product lesson / remaining question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **01 · Foundation** | Make the complete command loop playable. | Initial map, platform roles, deployment and simulation foundation. | A feature list needs a coherent deploy-to-outcome loop. Can new players complete it? |
| **02 · Combined arms** | Broaden strategic choice. | Ground/naval roles and expansion to 15 operations. | Variety increases learning and balance demands. Does each scenario change a decision? |
| **03 · Direct command** | Reduce interaction friction and improve recognition. | Single-click attacks, richer unit presentation and 24 fictional airfields. | Faster input needs clear selection and order feedback. |
| **04 · Dynamic theatre** | Make the map and supporting systems matter. | 3D relief, moving fronts, drones, diplomacy and 29 player-deployable roles. | More systems must remain understandable during play. |
| **05 · Command scale** | Combine a bigger theatre with simpler access. | 20× playable land area, 3× physical movement, command views, pinned Forces & Build, drag navigation and crossing highlights. | Scale is a pacing decision. Check waiting time and orientation with players. |

## What changed in the product approach

The sequence moved from completeness, to breadth, to interaction simplification, to richer world behaviour, then to discoverability at scale. The important next step is evidence: first-session observation, interaction QA and scenario pacing checks before another large content expansion.

See [validation](../docs/VALIDATION.md) for what was actually checked and [roadmap](../docs/ROADMAP.md) for what remains.
